Omar Junior is the son of Omar del Diego de la Paz Senior (better known as Omar Senior) and Ayako Nagatomi.
He was born on September 4, 1962, in Tokyo.
He never knew his father, who, according to his mother, left the family home shortly after his birth.
Raised by his mother in Tokyo according to the strictest Japanese traditions, he seemed destined for a future as an itamae (master sushi chef).
At the age of 16, thanks to his mother's recommendations, he joined the team of Jiro Ono, Japan's greatest sushi master.
Omar did not know it, but his father had been one of the master's most loyal customers.
Training to become a master sushi chef is supposed to take at least ten years, and he would not even begin making sushi until after two or three years of apprenticeship.
For the time being, he had to content himself with mastering the subtle art of cooking rice.
To make matters worse, he was quickly ostracized by the master's other disciples, who could not understand what he was doing on the team because of his mixed heritage, which made him a gaijin in their eyes.
While he was still only a kitchen assistant, one evening, in an attempt to impress his fellow apprentices, he decided to prepare a fugu, a fish that can prove fatal if improperly prepared.
After slicing the fish into sashimi, he ate it under the amused gaze of his companions.
Unfortunately for him, it turned out that he had prepared the fish incorrectly, and he became seriously ill.
Coming close to death by asphyxiation due to paralysis of the respiratory tract, he miraculously survived but fell into a long coma.
He was transferred to the intensive care unit at Tokyo University Hospital.
A nurse named Ricky Brown took care of Omar throughout this period.
He too was a gaijin.
He was also regarded as something of an eccentric by the rest of the staff.
Indeed, he had a habit of roller-skating through the wing where all the patients in indefinite comas were kept.
American by birth, another of his peculiarities was that he always carried a small radio cassette player clipped to his belt as he roamed the corridors.
He listened exclusively to James Brown, and between roller-skating dance routines in the middle of the ward, he endlessly told his peaceful patients—none of whom were in any condition to contradict him—that he was none other than the grandson of the “Godfather of Soul”.
On June 6, 1979, after several months in a coma, Omar finally woke up.
The first person he saw when he opened his eyes was his mother, who had come to visit him.
His first words to her were, “Where is my father?”
Completely caught off guard, his mother replied that she had no idea whatsoever.
She added that the only thing Omar Senior had left her before disappearing was an old Spanish-made acoustic guitar.
Omar immediately became determined to find his father.
Abandoning his culinary studies on the spot, much to his mother's despair, he began spending his days around the port of Tokyo, looking for a job aboard a ship so that he could set off across the world at minimal expense.
Hanging around the port's seedy dives, crowded with crooks and sailors, and thanks to his remarkable tolerance for alcohol—particularly tequila and mezcal—he built himself a solid reputation and eventually became the mascot of the crew of a cruise ship, the MV Turkmenia, which hired him as a deckhand and assistant engineer.
Taking with him nothing but one change of clothes and his father's guitar, he left Japan for good, promising his mother that he would send her news as often as he possibly could.
And so Omar began a journey that would take him to all four corners of the globe.
Far from the austere life he had known in Japan, Omar discovered the world and the extraordinary variety of people who inhabited it, moving from encounter to encounter, ship to ship, country to country.
On board, every passenger wanted him at their table.
Every dinner inevitably ended in a drinking session from which he emerged as the sole survivor.
One evening, an Arab emir begged Omar to accompany him to his luxurious residence so that he could show him his collection of Rolls-Royces, thoroughbred horses, and his harem of more than sixty women.
Omar accepted, and five days later the emir flew him back by helicopter to the ship, which had already resumed its voyage.
During a stopover in Casablanca, he met Yves Saint Laurent, who was on his way to his villa in Marrakech.
Utterly fascinated by Omar's beauty and personality, he invited him to spend a few days with him.
Until then, Yves Saint Laurent had designed exclusively for women, but he was considering launching a men's line for his upcoming Spring/Summer 1982 collection and begged Omar to model for him in Paris.
Omar declined, however, as his current ship was about to set sail for the Americas.
Yves Saint Laurent subsequently abandoned the idea of working in menswear altogether.
In 1983, during a stopover in Nassau, Omar met the crew shooting the latest James Bond film, Never Say Never Again.
Rumor even has it that, with Sean Connery's complicity, he engaged in some rather extensive flirting with Kim Basinger, the film's Bond girl.
But Omar met his true love two years later in the person of Angela Grifeo di Partanna, a young and wealthy heiress two years his senior from a prominent Italian family.
He met her during an underground poker game organized by the chief engineer of the prestigious ocean liner S/S Norway during a Caribbean cruise.
The game, which carried an exorbitant buy-in, took place in a small office adjoining the engine room and brought together a handpicked group of extremely wealthy passengers.
Omar, whose relatively modest means did not allow him to pay the entrance fee, was sponsored by one of his friends, the wealthy Greek shipping magnate Stavros Niárchos.
After a fiercely contested game that lasted well into the night, Omar and Angela found themselves facing each other in the final showdown.
Angela eventually prevailed after a memorable hand that Omar had been absolutely certain he would win.
Although devastated by his crushing defeat, Omar was mesmerized by the charms of the young Italian aristocrat.
And he did not lose everything, as Angela invited him to spend the rest of the night in her cabin.
Omar would never truly recover from this encounter.
Despite numerous attempts, he would never manage to rid himself of the ghost of Angela, who would haunt his romantic life for years to come.
At first, fate brought them together again on several occasions at society parties where, invariably, the passion between them flared up as intensely as it had on the first day, only to die abruptly the following morning when Angela once again vanished without a trace.
Their encounters then became increasingly rare, before eventually ceasing altogether, much to Omar's despair.
On September 10, 1986, after seven years spent roaming the ports of the world, the Sea Goddess docked in Acapulco, Mexico, the final destination of its cruise before returning to Los Angeles, its home port.
Omar had decided to take a few days to explore this country, which, strangely enough, he had never really visited before and which was still buzzing from the World Cup that had shaken both Mexico and the rest of the planet that summer.
Immediately captivated by the electric atmosphere, the enthusiasm of the population, and the beauty of the local women, he decided not to return to L.A. and instead to remain in this new country, which exerted a powerful attraction over him that he could not explain.
He handed in his resignation to the ship's captain, bringing seven years of maritime wandering to an end.
After a farewell party worthy of the emotions shared by everyone present—and of the quality of the local tequila consumed by the liter that evening—Omar fell asleep on the beach as the Sea Goddess disappeared over the horizon, bound for new adventures.
For years afterward, still accompanied by his guitar, Omar followed the trail of his father across this dry and untamed country.
In one place, he would hear that his father had once been a legendary lucha libre wrestler; elsewhere, that he had been the right-hand man of a powerful cartel boss.
Eventually, however, he was forced to conclude that he would never find his father and that Omar Senior was probably dead.
Discouraged by this grim realization, he decided to settle in Puerto Escondido, more precisely on Zicatela Beach, a spot overrun with surfers and hippies from all over the world.
There he was introduced to the limitless world offered by natural psychedelic drugs, which allowed him to satisfy his thirst for travel without ever having to leave the small shack he occupied alone on the beach.
Year after year, his charisma and intelligence attracted travelers in search of spirituality and new sensations.
He became a kind of hippie guru and opened a modern-day “mission,” where he welcomed guests for stays of wildly varying lengths under conditions that were Spartan, yet remarkably pleasant.
We met Omar under circumstances that we would prefer to keep secret during a trip to Mexico.
He is currently the owner of a guesthouse on the Pacific coast in Zihuatanejo, in the state of Guerrero, and has returned to Japanese cooking—although it should be noted that he has replaced sake on the menu with mezcal, his favorite drink.
His establishment has earned a reputation in certain circles for the nyotaimori evenings he regularly organizes with the help of local young women, under the watchful, if benevolent, eye of Angela, who, after several years without any contact, finally reappeared one fine morning on his doorstep.
